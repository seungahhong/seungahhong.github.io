10:T652a,<h2 id="1-what-is-this-post-about">1. What Is This Post About?<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#1-what-is-this-post-about"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h2>
<p>Here it is in a single sentence.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>This is a story about <strong>a tool that, when the AI keeps making the same mistake, fixes the AI's configuration files on its own to prevent that mistake.</strong> With one catch: before it actually changes any file, it always asks a human, "Is it okay to fix it this way?"</p>
</blockquote>
<h3 id="first-what-is-a-harness">First, What Is a "Harness"?<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#first-what-is-a-harness"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h3>
<p>A <strong>harness</strong> is the collective name for <em>the configuration and code that surrounds an AI</em>. Think of it as a bundle of documents and rules that tell the AI, "In our project, work like this." In terms of Claude Code, these are usually the following files.</p>






























<table><thead><tr><th>File</th><th>What it does</th><th>Analogy</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>CLAUDE.md</td><td>Rules for the whole project</td><td>Company-wide handbook</td></tr><tr><td>SKILL.md</td><td>Specialized instructions for a specific task (e.g., web accessibility)</td><td>Field-specific manual</td></tr><tr><td>agents/*.md</td><td>Definitions of role-specific helper AIs</td><td>Team member job descriptions</td></tr><tr><td>hooks/*.sh</td><td>Scripts that run automatically at specific moments</td><td>Automatic alert/block device</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>The previous post, <a href="/blog/2026/06/2026-06-04-fe-harness/">Frontend Harness Engineering</a>, covered <strong>how to build these harnesses well.</strong> This post goes one step further: <strong>how to make a harness fix itself.</strong></p>
<h3 id="the-inconvenience-of-a-static-harness">The Inconvenience of a Static Harness<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#the-inconvenience-of-a-static-harness"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h3>
<p>Even a well-built harness, once created, just <strong>sits there frozen</strong> in place. Even when the AI repeats the same mistake, the harness cannot learn on its own. So every time, a human has to step in like this.</p>
<figure data-rehype-pretty-code-figure=""><pre tabindex="0" data-language="markdown" data-theme="github-light-high-contrast github-dark-high-contrast"><code data-language="markdown" data-theme="github-light-high-contrast github-dark-high-contrast" style="display: grid;"><span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">AI: (makes the same mistake again)</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">Me: "I already told you, don't do it this way..."</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">Me: (open SKILL.md myself and add a rule)</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">   → next time, yet another mistake → repeat again...</span></span></code></pre></figure>
<p>Automating this repetition is the theme of this post.</p>






























<table><thead><tr><th>Situation</th><th>Existing (static) harness</th><th>Self-healing harness</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>AI makes the same mistake again</td><td>A human points it out every time</td><td>Records that moment automatically</td></tr><tr><td>"Why did it happen?" root-cause analysis</td><td>A human digs through the logs</td><td>The tool digs through the records and diagnoses the cause</td></tr><tr><td>Editing configuration files</td><td>A human edits them directly</td><td>The tool proposes an edit</td></tr><tr><td>Improvement history</td><td>Relies on human memory</td><td>Accumulates neatly as files</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>The core is just one thing: <strong>"a human fixes the harness" → "the harness prepares to fix itself, and a human only gives final approval."</strong></p>
<p>The tool that actually implements this is the star of this post, <strong>meta-harness</strong>. (Just as the name suggests, it is "a harness that fixes harnesses" — a meta harness.)</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="2-safety-first--three-promises">2. Safety First — Three Promises<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#2-safety-first--three-promises"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h2>
<p>The phrase "the AI fixes files on its own" sounds a little scary. What if it goes rogue? That is why meta-harness keeps <strong>three promises</strong> it never breaks. Once you understand these three, the rest is easy.</p>
<h3 id="promise-1--never-fix-anything-without-human-approval">Promise 1 — Never Fix Anything Without Human Approval<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#promise-1--never-fix-anything-without-human-approval"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h3>
<p>This is the most important one. meta-harness <strong>automates only the root-cause analysis and the "how about fixing it this way?" proposal</strong>, and <strong>the actual file edit happens only after a human says "OK."</strong></p>
<figure data-rehype-pretty-code-figure=""><pre tabindex="0" data-language="markdown" data-theme="github-light-high-contrast github-dark-high-contrast"><code data-language="markdown" data-theme="github-light-high-contrast github-dark-high-contrast" style="display: grid;"><span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">Record the mistake  →  Diagnose the cause  →  Draft the fix  ──┐</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">                                                              │  automated up to here only</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">        ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">        ▼</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">   [ Ask the human ]   "Fix #1? What about #2?"</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">        │</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">        ▼  only the ones approved</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">   Actual file edit</span></span></code></pre></figure>
<p>Approval can be given item by item.</p>
<ul>
<li>"Apply #1" → applied</li>
<li>"Don't do #2, I did that on purpose" → rejected</li>
<li>"I'll look at #3 later" → deferred</li>
</ul>
<p>For items you do not approve, <strong>not a single character of any file is touched.</strong></p>
<h3 id="promise-2--dont-break-other-things-while-fixing">Promise 2 — Don't Break Other Things While Fixing<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#promise-2--dont-break-other-things-while-fixing"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h3>
<p>There is a common trap: <strong>rules becoming too long and complex in an effort to raise accuracy.</strong> Before adopting a fix, meta-harness <strong>checks four things at once.</strong> If even one of them gets worse, that fix is discarded.</p>

























<table><thead><tr><th>Check item</th><th>Plain explanation</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><strong>Consistency</strong></td><td>Does the AI actually behave the way we want?</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Body cost</strong></td><td>Do the rules get needlessly long? (shorter is better)</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Trigger accuracy</strong></td><td>Does the rule fire only in exactly the right situations?</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Generalization</strong></td><td>Does it also work well in other similar situations?</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>And the way it edits is conservative too. It <strong>prefers to "add" only the necessary rules without deleting any existing lines.</strong> (This is called the additive approach.)</p>
<h3 id="promise-3--keep-the-original-instead-of-summarizing">Promise 3 — Keep the Original Instead of Summarizing<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#promise-3--keep-the-original-instead-of-summarizing"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h3>
<p>It saves the record of the moment the AI made a mistake <strong>whole, without summarizing.</strong> Because the moment you summarize, the clues to the real cause disappear. An experiment in the paper meta-harness draws on backs this up.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>The side that preserved the full original outperformed the summarized version — <strong>56.7 vs. 38.7 points</strong></p>
</blockquote>
<p>So even when diagnosing, instead of a "summary feel," it digs directly through the original records and pinpoints the evidence, saying things like <strong>"the problem occurred at step N, in file X."</strong></p>
<hr>
<h2 id="3-how-does-it-actually-work">3. How Does It Actually Work?<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#3-how-does-it-actually-work"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h2>
<p>You don't need to memorize any special commands for meta-harness. <strong>Just ask in your ordinary tone of voice</strong> and it fires on its own. Internally, it flows like this. Don't be intimidated — the three promises above are simply woven throughout this flow.</p>
<figure data-rehype-pretty-code-figure=""><pre tabindex="0" data-language="markdown" data-theme="github-light-high-contrast github-dark-high-contrast"><code data-language="markdown" data-theme="github-light-high-contrast github-dark-high-contrast" style="display: grid;"><span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">① Assess situation   Automatically judge whether this is a first run or a continuation</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">② What to fix        Determine "what kind of request is this?"</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">③ Record the mistake Save the problem moment (the utterance + the immediately preceding output) verbatim</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">④ Diagnose the cause "Why did this happen?" — analyze multiple mistakes at once</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">⑤ Draft the fixes    "Fix it like this" — one per mistake</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">⑥ Quick check        Filter out obvious problems like typos and broken links in advance</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">⑦ Confirm with human ★ "Fix #1? What about #2?" (always goes through this)</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">⑧ Actually apply     Edit only the files approved</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">⑨ Save the history   Record what was fixed this time</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">   └─ Finally: report in a table what / why / where was fixed</span></span></code></pre></figure>
<h3 id="helper-ais-that-divide-up-the-work">Helper AIs That Divide Up the Work<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#helper-ais-that-divide-up-the-work"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h3>
<p>meta-harness doesn't do everything alone; it splits up the roles. Just like a small team.</p>






























<table><thead><tr><th>Role</th><th>Name</th><th>What it does</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Recording</td><td>trace-capturer</td><td>Save the problem moment verbatim</td></tr><tr><td>Diagnosis</td><td>failure-diagnostician</td><td>Find the true cause of each individual mistake</td></tr><tr><td>Fixing</td><td>pareto-refiner</td><td>Draft fixes that match the causes</td></tr><tr><td>Bookkeeping</td><td>experience-historian</td><td>Organize and archive the improvement history as files</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p><strong>Diagnosis runs several in parallel</strong> (since the mistakes are independent of each other), while <strong>fixes proceed one at a time in sequence.</strong> That's because each fix has to be crafted while watching the result of the previous one.</p>
<h3 id="three-ways-to-ask-r1--r2--r3">Three Ways to Ask (R1 / R2 / R3)<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#three-ways-to-ask-r1--r2--r3"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h3>
<p>Depending on how you phrase it, the scope of the fix changes.</p>
<ul>
<li>
<p><strong>R1 — Prevent this mistake right now</strong></p>
<blockquote>
<p>"Redo the last one. Look at why it happened, and fix the rules you were using and CLAUDE.md."
→ Touches the project rules and the manual you were just using.</p>
</blockquote>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>R2 — Fix this plugin itself</strong></p>
<blockquote>
<p>"This tool keeps behaving oddly. Go into its internal settings and fix it."
→ Touches all of the plugin's internal files.</p>
</blockquote>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>R3 — Find the culprit that produced this document and fix it</strong></p>
<blockquote>
<p>"This spec.md is flimsy — fix the AI/manual that produced it."
→ Traces the document backward and fixes the cause that produced it.</p>
</blockquote>
</li>
</ul>
<hr>
<h2 id="4-it-remembers-on-its-own-even-without-commands">4. It Remembers on Its Own, Even Without Commands<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#4-it-remembers-on-its-own-even-without-commands"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h2>
<p>This is the part that lives up to the name "self-healing." Even without calling the tool directly every time, your <strong>complaints and fix requests accumulate automatically.</strong></p>
<figure data-rehype-pretty-code-figure=""><pre tabindex="0" data-language="markdown" data-theme="github-light-high-contrast github-dark-high-contrast"><code data-language="markdown" data-theme="github-light-high-contrast github-dark-high-contrast" style="display: grid;"><span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">[ Today's session ]  "redo this / that's wrong / change direction"</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">    │</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">    ▼  auto-detected (silently just recorded to a file, without blocking the flow)</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">    signals/2026-07-05.jsonl  ← saves your words verbatim</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">    │</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">    ┈┈┈┈┈┈┈  (a few days later, a new session)  ┈┈┈┈┈┈┈</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">    │</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">[ Next session ]  a one-line notice right at the start:</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">    "N recent self-heal signals have piled up — want to diagnose now?"</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">    │</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">    ▼  if you say "yes"</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#0E1116;--shiki-dark:#F0F3F6">    pulls out the accumulated records and starts root-cause analysis</span></span></code></pre></figure>
<p>An important point here: what it does automatically goes only <strong>as far as "recording" and "notifying."</strong> The actual fix still goes through <strong>Promise 1 (human approval).</strong> In other words, <em>"it remembers and fixes later"</em> is accurate, but that fix <strong>always happens together with human approval.</strong> It never fixes anything in secret.</p>
<h3 id="wait--why-use-an-automatic-script-hook-instead-of-a-rule">Wait — Why Use an "Automatic Script (hook)" Instead of a "Rule"?<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#wait--why-use-an-automatic-script-hook-instead-of-a-rule"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h3>
<p>Even to block the same thing, where you put it depends on its nature.</p>





















<table><thead><tr><th>Nature</th><th>Where to put it</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><strong>Must be blocked unconditionally</strong> (e.g., blocking dangerous commands)</td><td>In an automatic script (hook) or permission</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Only needs to be recorded</strong></td><td>Automatic script (silently records)</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Requires judgment</strong></td><td>As guidance text in a document (<code>CLAUDE.md</code> / <code>SKILL.md</code>)</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>The point is this: <strong>if you write down "something that must absolutely be blocked" only as prose in a document, the AI can miss it, so you block it firmly with an automatic script from the start.</strong> Document rules are, at best, "recommendations"; the enforcement comes from hooks and permissions.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="5-a-real-example--it-finds-the-file-on-its-own-even-without-being-told">5. A Real Example — It Finds the File on Its Own, Even Without Being Told<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#5-a-real-example--it-finds-the-file-on-its-own-even-without-being-told"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h2>
<p>The most impressive scene is this: <strong>even without pointing out "fix this file,"</strong> meta-harness finds the place to fix on its own.</p>
<p><strong>What I said (no file mentioned):</strong></p>
<blockquote>
<p>"You forgot to attach a name tag (accessibility label) to the icon button again. Please fix this so I stop repeating this mistake."</p>
</blockquote>
<p><strong>What the tool did:</strong></p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Record</strong> — Save verbatim the output I just made (the button missing a label) and my complaint.</li>
<li><strong>Find the culprit</strong> — Since I didn't tell it which file, it <strong>searches through the project itself.</strong> It searches the manual files for keywords like icon button and accessibility label.</li>
<li><strong>Verdict</strong> — Concludes that the real culprit is the web accessibility manual (<code>a11y/SKILL.md</code>). It <strong>excludes</strong> other seemingly unrelated files <strong>from the candidates,</strong> giving reasons.</li>
<li><strong>Fix → confirm → apply</strong> — Draft the fix, ask the human, and apply if OK.</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>The applied fix (existing lines left as-is, only a rule added):</strong></p>
<figure data-rehype-pretty-code-figure=""><pre tabindex="0" data-language="diff" data-theme="github-light-high-contrast github-dark-high-contrast"><code data-language="diff" data-theme="github-light-high-contrast github-dark-high-contrast" style="display: grid;"><span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#024C1A;--shiki-dark:#72F088">+ - **Interactive element name check (required)** — verify that each button, link, and input field has a name tag.</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#024C1A;--shiki-dark:#72F088">+   - **Why:** an icon-only button has no text, so without a name tag</span></span>
<span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#024C1A;--shiki-dark:#72F088">+     a screen reader cannot read out "what button it is."</span></span></code></pre></figure>
<blockquote>
<p>In fact, without being told a single file, the tool <strong>scanned 27 manuals and pinpointed test/SKILL.md as the culprit exactly.</strong> On top of that, it identified the cause more precisely — not as "because there was no rule," but as <strong>"there is a rule, but it wasn't enforced at the moment of creation."</strong></p>
</blockquote>

<h3 id="fixing-the-same-spot-3-times--rethink-the-structure">Fixing the Same Spot 3 Times? — "Rethink the Structure"<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#fixing-the-same-spot-3-times--rethink-the-structure"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h3>
<p>When you find yourself fixing the same file over and over, the count goes up, and <strong>from the third time on,</strong> instead of touch-ups it recommends, <strong>"You'd be better off redesigning the structure of this part itself."</strong> It's a signal to stop just applying band-aids. It's also a safeguard that keeps self-improvement from endlessly piling on rules.</p>
<hr>
<h2 id="7-wrap-up--how-far-weve-come-and-where-were-going">7. Wrap-Up — How Far We've Come, and Where We're Going<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#7-wrap-up--how-far-weve-come-and-where-were-going"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h2>
<p>The harness is evolving in stages like this.</p>



































<table><thead><tr><th>Stage</th><th>Description</th><th>The human's role</th><th>Status</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><strong>Stage 1</strong> Static harness</td><td>Human designs, AI executes</td><td>Design + approval</td><td>Previous post (2026-06)</td></tr><tr><td><strong>Stage 2</strong> Self-diagnosis</td><td>AI finds the cause of mistakes on its own</td><td>Approve proposals</td><td><strong>meta-harness</strong></td></tr><tr><td><strong>Stage 3</strong> Self-improvement</td><td>AI even drafts the fixes</td><td>Set policy and boundaries</td><td><strong>meta-harness</strong></td></tr><tr><td><strong>Stage 4</strong> Autonomous agent</td><td>AI redesigns the flow itself</td><td>Set only the goal</td><td>Ahead</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>The core message is just one thing.</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>The AI improves itself, but the key to "whether it may be fixed" is always held by a human.</strong></p>
</blockquote>
<p>What plays the role of that key is the <strong>three promises</strong> we saw earlier — human approval, not breaking other things, and preserving the original. If the static harness helped with <em>"what to build,"</em> the self-healing harness automates <em>"how to make the harness itself better."</em> The developer sets only the goals and boundaries, and the system rolls the remaining improvements forward on its own, under approval.</p>
