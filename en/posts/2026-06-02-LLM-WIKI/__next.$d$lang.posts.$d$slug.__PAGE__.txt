11:T9200,<h3 id="overview">Overview<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#overview"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h3>
<p>LLM Wiki is a repository where you collect source materials such as planning documents, specs, and meeting notes, and Claude reads them, organizes their contents, and builds a single Korean-language wiki out of them. People take on the role of collecting materials and asking questions, while the actual work of writing and maintaining the wiki is done by Claude. It is a structure that adapts the "LLM Wiki" pattern proposed by Andrej Karpathy to Korean planning and knowledge management.</p>
<p>The conventional RAG approach searches the raw pile for an answer all over again every time a question comes in. Because organized results never accumulate, you keep digging through the same materials repeatedly. LLM Wiki organizes materials once at the moment they are added, turns them into a wiki, and answers questions from that wiki with sources attached. The key difference lies in "when knowledge gets organized."</p>
<p>By way of analogy, Obsidian is the workspace, the LLM is the author working inside it, the wiki is the finished deliverable, and the source materials are the requirements.</p>
<h3 id="background-why-build-it-this-way">Background: Why Build It This Way<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#background-why-build-it-this-way"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h3>
<p>Knowledge that piles up in work and study is usually scattered across several tools like Confluence, Slack, and Notion. To find what you need you have to hop between tools, and how the documents connect to one another rarely surfaces clearly.</p>
<p>To pile up this scattered knowledge in one place and link it together, we chose the LLM Wiki approach instead of RAG, which re-digs through the sources for every question. How the two approaches differ, and the Karpathy idea underlying them, is laid out below in "Karpathy's LLM Wiki Concept."</p>
<h3 id="karpathys-llm-wiki-concept">Karpathy's LLM Wiki Concept<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#karpathys-llm-wiki-concept"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h3>
<h4 id="the-core-idea">The Core Idea<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#the-core-idea"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h4>
<h5 id="the-problem-with-the-conventional-rag-approach">The Problem with the Conventional RAG Approach<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#the-problem-with-the-conventional-rag-approach"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h5>
<p>RAG repeats the following every time a user asks a question. First it converts the source documents into vector embeddings, finds document chunks similar to the question, and passes those chunks to the LLM to produce an answer. Because this approach re-reads the sources every time, search accuracy drops as the documents grow, and even when it does find each document chunk, the context of how the documents connect to one another has to be re-established for every answer, so it is easily lost.</p>
<h5 id="how-the-llm-wiki-approach-solves-it">How the LLM Wiki Approach Solves It<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#how-the-llm-wiki-approach-solves-it"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h5>
<p>LLM Wiki moves the moment of organizing forward to when materials are added. When a material is added, the LLM reads and organizes it once and "compiles" it into the wiki, and from then on, when a question is asked, it answers directly from the already-organized and cross-referenced wiki. In library terms, RAG is like going to the shelves yourself to find and read the original book every time you have a question, whereas LLM Wiki is like a dedicated librarian who reads each new book as it arrives, organizes it into the wiki, and pre-links related documents to one another.</p>
<p>Comparing the two approaches by the timing of organization looks like this. The key difference lies in "when knowledge gets organized."</p>

<h4 id="the-three-layer-architecture">The Three-Layer Architecture<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#the-three-layer-architecture"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h4>
<p>LLM Wiki consists of three layers. Each layer, likened to a library, is as follows.</p>

























<table><thead><tr><th>Layer</th><th>Description</th><th>Analogy</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Source materials (Raw)</td><td>Original documents the user adds. <p>For the actual folder layout, see "Repository Structure" below.</p>
<h4 id="three-core-operations">Three Core Operations<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#three-core-operations"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h4>
<p>The wiki runs on three operations: adding materials (Ingest), asking questions (Query), and inspecting (Lint). In each operation, the roles of the person and Claude are divided.</p>

























<table><thead><tr><th>Operation</th><th>Description</th><th>Division of roles</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Ingest (adding materials)</td><td>Reads new materials and organizes them into wiki pages</td><td>The user adds the material and Claude organizes it</td></tr><tr><td>Query (asking questions)</td><td>Finds the answer in the wiki and responds with sources</td><td>The user asks and Claude answers grounded in the wiki</td></tr><tr><td>Lint (health check)</td><td>Inspects the wiki's consistency and completeness</td><td>Claude inspects and cleans up after user approval</td></tr></tbody></table>
<h3 id="repository-structure">Repository Structure<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#repository-structure"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h3>
<p>It is divided into three layers.</p>





























<table><thead><tr><th>Location</th><th>Role</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><code>raw/</code></td><td>Where source materials are kept as-is. Planning documents, specs, meeting notes, images, URLs, and so on. It is an immutable area that is never changed once it comes in, and the user manages it. Claude only reads it.</td></tr><tr><td><code>wiki/</code></td><td>Where organized wiki pages collect. Claude writes and maintains it. People use it primarily in a read-oriented way. It is kept in a flat structure with no subfolders.</td></tr><tr><td><code>wiki/index.md</code></td><td>The full table of contents for the wiki. Organizes pages by category.</td></tr><tr><td><code>wiki/log.md</code></td><td>The work history. A line accumulates each time the wiki changes.</td></tr><tr><td><code>CLAUDE.md</code></td><td>The operating-rules document. Defines the flow for adding materials (Ingest), asking questions (Query), and inspecting (Lint), as well as the page-writing rules.</td></tr></tbody></table>
<h3 id="how-it-works">How It Works<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#how-it-works"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h3>
<p>Turning materials into a wiki goes through three working modes.</p>
<h4 id="adding-materials-ingest">Adding Materials (Ingest)<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#adding-materials-ingest"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h4>
<p>This is the process of reflecting new materials into the wiki. Claude reads the source, first shows the user two or three key summaries, then checks for duplication and either creates a new page or augments an existing one. At this point it does not arbitrarily rewrite the original sentences; it keeps only the summary set apart at the very top of the page. Related pages are connected to one another with wikilinks, and the work is recorded together in the table of contents and the history.</p>
<h4 id="asking-questions-query">Asking Questions (Query)<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#asking-questions-query"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h4>
<p>This is the process of getting an answer grounded in what is organized in the wiki. Claude finds and reads the relevant pages from the table of contents and answers with sources, grounded solely in what is written in the wiki. If there is no basis in the wiki, it does not make things up and clearly tells you "There is no such content in the wiki."</p>
<h4 id="inspecting-lint">Inspecting (Lint)<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#inspecting-lint"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h4>
<p>This is the process of periodically cleaning up problems that arise as the wiki accumulates. It finds and reports orphan pages that are not linked from anywhere, broken links, duplicated content, contradictions between pages, and so on, and cleans them up upon approval.</p>

<h3 id="core-operating-rules">Core Operating Rules<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#core-operating-rules"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h3>
<p>To preserve the wiki's trustworthiness, the following rules are followed.</p>
<ul>
<li>The <code>raw/</code> folder is only read by Claude and is never modified.</li>
<li>Every wiki page starts with a key summary (Takeaway).</li>
<li>Every time the wiki is modified, a record is left in <code>log.md</code>.</li>
<li>Newly added pages must be registered in the <code>index.md</code> table of contents. This is to prevent orphan pages that are not linked from anywhere.</li>
<li>Answers are grounded solely in what is written in the wiki. If there is no basis, it answers that there is none.</li>
</ul>
<h3 id="what-the-user-needs-to-do">What the User Needs to Do<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#what-the-user-needs-to-do"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h3>
<p>The user's role in this repository is clear. It is not to write the wiki by hand, but to collect good materials and pose accurate questions. Leave the writing and organizing of the wiki to Claude, and the user can work in the following flow.</p>
<ol>
<li>Put the source materials you want organized into the <code>raw/</code> folder. You can place files directly, or pass a web address as-is. Put images or attachments under <code>raw/assets/</code>.</li>
<li>Once you have added the materials, ask for them to be reflected into the wiki. For example, "Organize <code>raw/meeting-notes.md</code> into the wiki," or you can pass along a URL. When Claude first shows you the key summary, do a light check that the direction is right.</li>
<li>Ask about anything you are curious about as a question. An answer comes back with sources, within the scope of what is organized in the wiki. If the answer says there is no basis, you can add more relevant sources to <code>raw/</code>.</li>
<li>Once the wiki has accumulated to some degree, request an inspection now and then. You can clean up broken links, duplicates, and contradictions to keep the wiki healthy.</li>
</ol>
<p>There are two things worth keeping in mind while working. First, the materials in <code>raw/</code> are the source of truth, so the user manages them and Claude does not change them arbitrarily. If the wiki content and the source diverge, the source takes precedence. Second, it is better to leave edits to wiki pages to Claude rather than fixing them by hand, because it is designed so that every change is left in the history and can be traced.</p>
<h3 id="use-cases">Use Cases<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#use-cases"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h3>
<p><strong>Onboarding guide</strong> — If you gather scattered onboarding materials into <code>raw/</code> and organize them into a wiki, new hires can quickly find what they need by asking the wiki. When the source changes, the wiki is updated along with it.</p>
<p><strong>Standards-document management</strong> — Managing team standards, processes, planning documents, and technical specs as a wiki lets you maintain cross-references and consistency between documents. Regular inspections (Lint) catch broken links, duplicated content, and missing cross-references.</p>
<h3 id="scale-and-growth">Scale and Growth<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#scale-and-growth"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h3>
<p>Up to around 100 materials and several hundred pages, the table of contents and search alone are enough to handle it. Beyond that, you can optionally attach an auxiliary layer that visualizes and navigates the relationships between pages as a graph.</p>
<h3 id="extending-to-a-knowledge-graph-graphify">Extending to a Knowledge Graph (graphify)<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#extending-to-a-knowledge-graph-graphify"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h3>
<p>When the wiki has grown large enough that you want to see the relationships between pages at a glance, you attach graphify. graphify is a tool that analyzes the documents in a folder, extracts concepts and relationships, and builds a queryable knowledge graph (JSON, HTML visualization, report).</p>
<h4 id="the-extension-flow">The Extension Flow<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#the-extension-flow"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h4>
<p>When graphify converts a wiki built by Ingest into a graph, graph traversal is added on top of the existing text search and wikilink navigation.</p>

<p>The difference between using only a text wiki and adding graphify is as follows.</p>



































<table><thead><tr><th>Item</th><th>Wiki only</th><th>Wiki + graphify</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Search method</td><td>Keyword matching and following wikilinks</td><td>Graph traversal (BFS/DFS) and shortest-path queries</td></tr><tr><td>Relationship discovery</td><td>The person has to create cross-references by hand</td><td>Automatically extracts relationships and even proposes inferred ones</td></tr><tr><td>Grasping the whole structure</td><td>Only category level via the <code>index.md</code> table of contents</td><td>Visualizes the entire structure with an interactive HTML graph</td></tr><tr><td>Hidden connections</td><td>The person has to find them by hand</td><td>Automatic grouping via community detection</td></tr><tr><td>"The relationship between A and B"</td><td>Search each and connect if there is a cross-reference</td><td>Check the shortest path directly with the path command</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>graphify attaches a confidence level to each relationship it extracts. It distinguishes relationships clearly stated in the source as EXTRACTED (1.0), reasonably inferred relationships as INFERRED (0.65–0.95), and uncertain relationships as AMBIGUOUS (0.1–0.3), so you can tell where the stated facts end and inference begins.</p>
<p>The main commands used within a session are as follows.</p>





























<table><thead><tr><th>Command</th><th>Role</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><code>/graphify ./wiki</code></td><td>Generate a graph from the wiki/ folder (output: graphify-out/)</td></tr><tr><td><code>/graphify ./wiki --update</code></td><td>Incrementally update only the changed files</td></tr><tr><td><code>/graphify query "question"</code></td><td>Graph-based query</td></tr><tr><td><code>/graphify path "A" "B"</code></td><td>Shortest path between two concepts</td></tr><tr><td><code>/graphify explain "concept"</code></td><td>Explain a specific concept</td></tr></tbody></table>
<p>Graph generation and querying are handled directly by the Claude in the Claude Code session, so no separate API key is needed. For installation instructions, see the knowledge-graph extension item under "Installation and Getting Started" below.</p>

<h3 id="installation-and-getting-started">Installation and Getting Started<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#installation-and-getting-started"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h3>
<p>The repository address is as follows.</p>
<p><a href="https://github.com/seungahhong/llm-wiki">https://github.com/seungahhong/llm-wiki</a></p>
<p>This project runs on top of Claude Code. All the work of writing and organizing the wiki happens inside the Claude Code session, so you can pull the repository and use it right away without a separate server or build process.</p>
<h4 id="prerequisites">Prerequisites<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#prerequisites"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h4>
<ul>
<li>Git</li>
<li>Claude Code (Anthropic's official CLI). It is an installable tool, and you just need to log in once.</li>
<li>(Optional) Python 3.10 or higher and uv. Only needed when using the knowledge-graph extension feature.</li>
</ul>
<h4 id="installation-steps">Installation Steps<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#installation-steps"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h4>
<ol>
<li>Download the repository.</li>
</ol>

<ol start="2">
<li>Run Claude Code from the downloaded folder.</li>
</ol>
<figure data-rehype-pretty-code-figure=""><pre tabindex="0" data-language="bash" data-theme="github-light-high-contrast github-dark-high-contrast"><code data-language="bash" data-theme="github-light-high-contrast github-dark-high-contrast" style="display: grid;"><span data-line=""><span style="--shiki-light:#702C00;--shiki-dark:#FFB757">claude</span></span></code></pre></figure>

<p>Once you are ready, just start by putting the materials you want organized into the <code>raw/</code> folder.</p>
<h4 id="basic-usage-flow">Basic Usage Flow<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#basic-usage-flow"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h4>
<p>Once you have opened the repository, you use it by entering the following commands into the Claude Code prompt. These are not ordinary terminal commands but slash commands you enter within the session.</p>





















<table><thead><tr><th>Command</th><th>Role</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><code>/ingest raw/filename</code> or <code>/ingest URL</code></td><td>Reads the source material and organizes it into a wiki page.</td></tr><tr><td><code>/query question text</code></td><td>Answers with sources, grounded in the organized wiki.</td></tr><tr><td><code>/lint</code></td><td>Inspects for orphan pages, broken links, duplicates, and contradictions.</td></tr></tbody></table>

<p>Install this only when the wiki has grown large enough that you want to see the relationships between pages as a graph. It is not needed for everyday use.</p>

<h4 id="notes">Notes<a class="heading-anchor" aria-hidden="true" tabindex="-1" href="#notes"><span class="icon icon-link"></span></a></h4>
<ul>
<li>The source materials in the <code>raw/</code> folder are the source of truth. The user manages them, and if they diverge from the wiki content, the source takes precedence.</li>
<li>Local-only configuration files (<code>.claude/settings.json</code>, etc.) and graph outputs (<code>graphify-out/</code>) are not included in the repository. To use the graph extension in a freshly pulled folder, just run the installation commands above again.</li>
</ul>
